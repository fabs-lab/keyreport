Regular fees did not cover regular costs in most years

Needs review

The Income section of these statements includes reserve draws and special assessments, which are money brought in to cover costs rather than regular fee revenue. Setting those aside, and also removing reimbursement pairs (insurance proceeds against claim repairs, loan collections against loan charges) from both sides, recurring costs exceeded recurring fees in: 2021 −$46,488, 2022 −$201,618, 2023 −$499,872, 2024 −$341,473, 2025 −$272,965. The largest gap is 2023, where recurring fees of $1,468,291 met recurring costs of $1,968,163, drawing $313,945 of funding inflows — Transfers from reserves $222,478; Special assessments $91,467. Reported totals look positive in 2021, 2025 only because those funding inflows are counted as income. Confirm which draws the board approved and for what purpose.

Budget-vs-actual comparisons are not reliable in every year

Needs review

Many statement rows carry an actual amount with no budget figure, so an unadjusted comparison against budget understates the denominator and looks like an overrun. 2023: 80.7% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($394,256 without one); 2024: 69.9% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($624,295 without one); 2025: 94.2% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($147,512 without one); 2026: 69.7% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($369,624 without one). Percent-of-budget is therefore reported only for 2021, 2022, where coverage is at least 95%. Earlier apparent overruns in the low-coverage years should not be read as spending above an approved budget without checking the source statements.

Some large charges are account-to-account reclassifications, not new costs

Needs review

1 charge of $5,000 or more is offset by an equal credit on another account in the same month, so the net cost is $0. Largest: $142,250 in 2024-01, debited to Boiler Repairs & Replacement (7615) and credited from Boiler Repairs (7610). These are excluded from the unusually-large-charge table. Confirm the reason for each move, but do not read them as additional spending.
