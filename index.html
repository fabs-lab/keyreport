From the statements
  • Excluding the Insurance category (sensitivity), operating-style expenses rose from $1,564,794 in 2021 to $2,031,419 in 2025 (+$466,625, 29.8%).

  • Front desk & management added $117,017.
  • Maintenance contracts added $10,831.
  • Many recurring contract lines barely moved — for example Front Desk Personnel - Contract $197,773→$215,039, Reserve Contributions $128,948→$134,823, Management Fees $56,712→$62,515.
  • Offsetting decline: Other expenses -4,542 (-1.0% of the net change).
  • Offsetting decline: Utilities -6,389 (-1.4% of the net change).
  • Offsetting decline: Legal & professional -47,160 (-10.1% of the net change).

Regular fees did not cover regular costs in every full year (2021–2025)

Needs review
  • The Income section includes reserve draws and special assessments — money brought in to cover costs rather than regular fee revenue.
  • Setting those aside, and also removing reimbursement pairs (insurance proceeds against claim repairs, loan collections against loan charges) from both sides, recurring costs exceeded recurring fees in:
  • 2021: −$46,488.
  • 2022: −$201,618.
  • 2023: −$499,872.
  • 2024: −$341,473.
  • 2025: −$272,965.
  • Largest gap is 2023: recurring fees $1,468,291 vs recurring costs $1,968,163, drawing $313,945 of funding inflows — Transfers from reserves $222,478; Special assessments $91,467.
  • Reported net is positive in 2025 only after counting funding inflows as income.
  • In 2021, reported net stays positive after removing funding inflows alone; the regular-fee shortfall appears only after also removing matched reimbursement pairs from both sides.
  • Confirm which draws the board approved and for what purpose.

Budget-vs-actual comparisons are not reliable in every year

Needs review
  • Many statement rows carry an actual amount with no budget figure, so an unadjusted comparison against budget understates the denominator and looks like an overrun.
  • 2023: 80.7% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($394,256 without one).
  • 2024: 69.9% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($624,295 without one).
  • 2025: 94.2% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($147,512 without one).
  • 2026: 74.3% of expense dollars have a budget figure ($381,273 without one).
  • Percent-of-budget is therefore reported only for 2021, 2022, where coverage is at least 95%.
  • Earlier apparent overruns in the low-coverage years should not be read as spending above an approved budget without checking the source statements.

Some large charges are account-to-account reclassifications, not new costs

Needs review
  • 1 charge of $5,000 or more is offset by an equal credit on another account in the same month, so the net cost is $0.
  • Largest: $142,250 in 2024-01, debited to Boiler Repairs & Replacement (7615) and credited from Boiler Repairs (7610).
  • These are excluded from the unusually-large-charge table.
  • Confirm the reason for each move, but do not read them as additional spending.
